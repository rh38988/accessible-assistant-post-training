# Spot and avoid tech support scams

Warning signs:
- A pop-up, call, text, or email says your computer has a problem and tells you to call a number. Real security warnings never ask you to call a phone number.
- Legitimate tech companies do not contact you by phone, email, or text about a problem with your computer.
- The "technician" may say your accounts were hacked or that you are linked to a crime, then pass you to someone pretending to work for the government. No real government worker will tell you to move your money to "protect it."
- They ask you to pay with a gift card, wire transfer, bank transfer, cryptocurrency, or a payment app.
- A message says a subscription is renewing and to call a number. Do not use the number in the message.

What to do:
- Hang up, close the pop-up, and do not call the number shown.
- If you shared your username and password, change that password right away, and change it anywhere else you use the same one.
- If you think you were charged, check your bank or credit card statement and ask the bank or card company to reverse the charge.
- Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
